About this site

Why I built this site, how it was made, and how much you can trust it.

Why

I built this site for the symposium Research Prioritization in Dutch Metascience on 10 December 2026. Metascience in the Netherlands is done in many fields and at many institutions, by people who do not always know about each other's work. The goal of the site is to make it easier to see who works on what, and who you might want to talk to. It is meant as a source of inspiration, not as a piece of scholarship.

How it was made

The site shows metascience papers with at least one author at a Dutch institution: at the moment about 8,900 papers by about 9,600 people. I started from a list of Dutch metascientists and their publication lists, and added papers that a search of OpenAlex found for metascience terms. For each of about 63,000 candidate papers, a language model judged whether it is metascience. The papers that remained were sorted into 12 categories and 20 themes, and linked to the papers they cite, to NWO grants, and to public posts on Bluesky and Mastodon.

Almost all classification and data cleaning was done by AI: an open language model running on a GPU of SURF, and Claude, working as many separate reviewers who each followed written rules. I decided on those rules, checked the output, and had the rules changed when the output was wrong.

How much can you trust it?

Deciding what counts as metascience is not easy, and I do not think any automatic procedure gets it completely right. What I could do is check, find the errors, improve the rules, and check again, and I did this many times. The first automatic pass let through too many papers that are not metascience, so a stricter second pass followed. Every remaining paper was then read again (title and abstract), and the reviewers' quality was measured against papers whose answer had been decided beforehand, and by letting two reviewers judge the same papers independently. Borderline cases led to explicit rules, and all borderline and removed papers were judged again under those rules. When one of the original categories turned out to hold more than a third of all papers, I replaced the categories with twelve new ones, each with written borders to its neighbours. Retraction notices, corrections, retracted papers and duplicate versions of the same paper are left out. In total about 700 papers were removed by hand, each with a reason.

The result is useful, but not perfect. A few percent of the papers shown are probably not metascience, and metascience papers are missing, especially papers that are not in OpenAlex or that use none of the search terms. People are identified by OpenAlex, which sometimes splits one person into several, or merges two people with the same name. "Dutch" means a Dutch institution on the paper, so researchers who moved abroad still appear with their Dutch papers. The names of the 20 themes and the definitions of the 12 categories were suggested by AI and revised by me. Which category each paper belongs to was decided by AI reviewers following those definitions, and the short summary of each paper was written by AI and not checked; both can be wrong.

Privacy

The site only shows public information: names as they appear on papers, ORCID identifiers, institutions, titles, co-authorships, citations, NWO grants, and the AI judgements. "Find yourself" on the Overview runs in your browser; nothing is sent anywhere, because the site has no server. The Socials page uses public posts on Bluesky and Mastodon by people who work in the Netherlands now, and names only people who post regularly about metascience. If you want to be removed or corrected, or if a social media account was matched to you by mistake, please open an issue on GitHub.

More details

Every step is described in detail in the README of the GitHub repository, including the rules the AI reviewers followed and how their agreement was measured. All scripts are in the repository, and the data behind every chart are in docs/data/. OpenAlex data are CC0. Last built in November 2026.
