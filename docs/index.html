This website was built by Daniel Lakens for the symposium Research Prioritization in Dutch Metascience on 10 December. Its goal is to let attendees explore possible collaborations based on shared interests.

We rely on classifications by OpenAlex, metadata from NWO, keywords and ORCID data provided by authors, and data cleaning by Claude Opus 5.5. I have performed some basic quality checks, but it was neither possible nor needed for the preparation of the symposium to validate all data by hand. The data are provided for inspiration, not scholarship, and will contain errors and incorrect classifications.

Metascience in the Netherlands

Research on research: replication, research practices, open science, peer review, integrity, statistical inference, research evaluation and science policy. This site maps metascience papers with at least one author at a Dutch institution that we found in OpenAlex, as judged by a language model and checked by AI reviewers. Some papers are missing, and some papers shown are not metascience. Everything is interactive: hover for details, click legends to hide series, and follow the links to explore.

Find yourself

Work on metascience in the Netherlands? Choose your name to see the whole site from your position: your papers, co-authors, subfield, institution and grants are highlighted on every page. The choice is kept only in this browser; nothing is sent anywhere. All data on this site are public, so you can also choose anyone else.

How the field grew

Metascience papers with an author at a Dutch institution per year, per 1,000 of all publications with an author at a Dutch institution in OpenAlex (same kinds of work): whether the field grew faster than Dutch research as a whole. The last year is not complete yet.

Per category

By default each category's share of that year's metascience papers on this site: how the mix of the field changes. Switch to "per 1,000 Dutch publications" (all publications with an author at a Dutch institution in OpenAlex, same kinds of work) to see whether a category grew faster than Dutch research as a whole, or to the number of papers. Each panel has its own vertical scale, so that small categories stay visible: compare the shape of the curves, not their height (the number under each name gives the size). The last year is not complete yet.

The rise of terms

Papers on this site per year that use each term in their title or abstract, by default as a share of that year's metascience papers. Each panel has its own vertical scale, so that rare terms stay visible: compare the shape of the curves, not their height (the total under each term gives the size). Abstracts are missing for some papers, so the counts are a lower bound. The last year is not complete yet.

Rising and falling keywords

The rank of each keyword among the most frequent keywords in each period (1 = most frequent). The eight keywords that lead the most recent period are coloured; hover any line for its name.

Twenty themes, year by year

The papers fall into 20 clusters of similar content (from their abstracts); the colour shows how many papers each cluster had per year. See Themes for the map behind it.

Newcomers per year

The number of authors at Dutch institutions whose first metascience paper on this site appeared in that year.

Explore

  • Who works with whom: the co-authorship network, with communities and institutions.
  • People: each person's categories, timeline, co-authors and papers.
  • Papers: every paper with filters and details, what got cited, and the landmark papers per category.
  • Themes: a map of all papers, theme clusters, keywords and institutions × themes.
  • History: a timeline per theme of historical events (Wikipedia) and the landmark papers that the papers on this site cite most.
  • Places: Dutch institutions on a map, their collaborations, and partner countries.
  • Funding: NWO grants we could link to these researchers and the NWO projects we identified as metascience-related, with a timeline.
  • Inferences: who could work together, rising themes, and shared questions for joint projects.
Data: OpenAlex (CC0) and Crossref. Theme labels are AI judgements and can be wrong; see About.
