Metascience in the Netherlands

In preparation for the symposium 'Research Prioritization in Dutch Metascience' on 10 December, I made this website to describe who works on Metascience in The Netherlands and the themes Dutch metascientists study. The website contains a list of 8,899 papers (co-)authored by Dutch metascientists, an annotated history of important events in metascience per subfield, and an overview of funding from the Dutch science funder NWO. The 'Socials' page provides information on posts by Dutch metascientists on the open social media platforms Mastodon and Bluesky.

The goal of this site is to allow attendees of the symposium to explore possible collaborations based on shared interests. Some suggestions are provided in the 'Working Together' page.

Find yourself

Work on metascience in the Netherlands? Choose your name to see the whole site from your position: your papers, co-authors, subfield, institution and grants are highlighted on every page. The choice is kept only in this browser; nothing is sent anywhere. All data on this site are public, so you can also choose anyone else.

The map of the literature

Every dot is a metascience paper. Each paper's title and abstract were turned into a numeric fingerprint by a language model (bge-m3), and papers with similar fingerprints lie close together. The colours are the 12 categories; the names on the map are the 20 clusters of similar papers (described on Working Together). Click a category to turn it grey.

Scroll to zoom, drag to move, hover for the title, click a dot to open the paper. As you zoom in, the titles of the most cited papers appear. The positions only mean "similar to nearby papers"; the axes have no meaning.

Who works with whom

Every dot is an author at a Dutch institution of metascience papers on this site; a line means they wrote at least one paper together (papers with more than 30 authors are left out, so one large project does not tie hundreds of people together). Scroll to zoom, drag to move, hover for details. Click a person: their co-authors light up in the network, and their details and papers appear beside it. The colours show groups of people who write together more than with others (Louvain method on the co-authorship links). They are not formal groups and have no names; hover over a colour or a person to see the group's size and its two most common themes.

Twenty themes, year by year

The papers fall into 20 clusters of similar content (from their abstracts); the colour shows how many papers each cluster had per year. The clusters are named on the map above.

Explore

  • People: each person's categories, timeline, co-authors and papers.
  • Papers: every paper with filters and details, how the field grew, what got cited, and the landmark papers per category.
  • History: a timeline per theme of historical events (Wikipedia) and the landmark papers that the papers on this site cite most.
  • Funding: NWO grants we could link to these researchers and the NWO projects we identified as metascience-related, with a timeline.
  • Working Together: the 20 themes, who could work together, rising themes, and shared questions for joint projects.
Papers and authors: OpenAlex (CC0) and Crossref. The map groups papers by text fingerprints of their titles and abstracts. See About for data quality and AI judgments.
