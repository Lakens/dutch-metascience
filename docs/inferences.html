Inferences

What the data suggest, beyond describing them: who could work together, which themes are rising, and which shared questions could become joint projects. These are suggestions computed from titles and abstracts, not judgements about anyone's work; use them as a starting point for a conversation.

Who could work together?

For every active researcher (; people) we list the researchers whose papers are closest in content (and clearly alike: a similarity of at least ), but with whom they have written no metascience paper in these data, or only a few: at most of the papers of the one with fewer papers (one joint paper means little to someone with 100 papers, much to someone with 3). Papers with more than authors do not count as writing together. "Closest in content" uses the same text fingerprints as the map on the Themes page; each suggestion shows the clusters both work in and the two papers (one of each) that are most alike.

Communities that could talk more

Each circle is a research community: a group of people who mostly write together, numbered by size (group 1 is the largest; the same numbers as on Who works with whom), with circle size showing the number of people. Communities whose papers are alike in content lie close together (only approximately: the distances on the map correlate with the differences in content; hover over a line for the exact similarity). Grey lines are joint papers, thicker for more. Two circles close together with no line, or a thin one, work on similar themes but hardly write together; the dashed lines mark the five clearest cases, listed below the map.

Which themes are rising?

The yearly change in each theme's share of the metascience papers on this site over the last four years (), with a 95% interval. A theme is called rising or declining only when the whole interval lies above or below zero; otherwise the data cannot tell a change from year-to-year variation. A rising share means a theme gets relatively more attention, whatever the growth of the field as a whole. How this is estimated is explained below.

What is rising on social media?

The same question for the Bluesky and Mastodon posts of the people on this site who work in the Netherlands now (see Socials): each theme's or term's share of all metascience posts in the last two years (), divided by its share in the two years before (). A ratio of 2 means the subject takes up twice as large a part of the metascience posts as before. Shares of the metascience posts are used, not of all posts, because people now post much more about other things than in 2023. The 95% interval comes from resampling people, so that one very active person cannot decide the result; a subject is called rising or declining only when the whole interval lies above or below 1. Terms with fewer than 30 posts or fewer than 5 people are left out.

Which themes attract new people?

The number of authors at Dutch institutions who wrote their first paper on this site in a theme in , divided by the number in , with a 95% interval. A person can be new to metascience or come from another theme. The whole field grew, so the dashed line shows the ratio for all themes together: a theme to its right attracts new people faster than the field as a whole. Papers with more than authors are not counted, as for the co-author network.

How the trends were estimated

Similar ongoing projects

The metascience-related NWO projects that are running now or will start soon (started in 2023 or later, or ending in the future; NWO's open data give no end date for recent projects), grouped by shared aim. Only NWO grants are included, not ZonMw, EU or university grants. The groups were made in two steps: the projects were first grouped by the similarity of their titles and summaries (the same text fingerprints as for the papers), then Claude, an AI assistant, read every summary, corrected the groups and wrote the descriptions; a second Claude reviewer checked every group against the summaries (instructions: scripts/project_groups_instructions.md). Project leaders are linked when they are on this site. Under each project: the papers on this site closest in content, by the same fingerprints.
